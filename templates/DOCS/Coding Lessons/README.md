## The 9-Step Teaching Format

Every lesson file follows this structure. No step is optional.

### Step 1 — Approach and reasoning
What did we do, and why? Walk through the starting point and the reasoning chain. Not just *what* — the *why* behind every major choice.

### Step 2 — Roads not taken
What other approaches were considered and rejected? This is where the deepest learning lives. Every decision has alternatives — show them and explain what was wrong with them.

### Step 3 — How the pieces connect
How do the different parts of the work fit together? Explain why that order and structure makes sense.

### Step 4 — Tools, methods, and frameworks
What specific tools or patterns were used? Why those and not others?

### Step 5 — Tradeoffs
Every design decision sacrifices something to gain something else. Show both sides explicitly.

### Step 6 — Mistakes, dead ends, and wrong turns
What didn't work? What had to be corrected? The mess is where the learning lives.

### Step 7 — Pitfalls to watch for
The "I wish someone told me this earlier" section. Concrete warnings for anyone doing something similar.

### Step 8 — What an expert notices
What separates good thinking from average thinking here? What would a senior engineer see that a junior would miss?

### Step 9 — Transferable lessons
How do the ideas from this task apply to completely different problems? Connect the dots.

---

## Tone rules

- Write like you're explaining over coffee, not writing documentation
- Use analogies, short stories, and real-world comparisons
- If a concept is abstract, make it concrete
- No jargon without explanation
- The reader should finish feeling like they *understand what happened and why* — not just see the final result
